How I build

Five things I insist on when AI meets professional users.

Counsellors are accountable for the advice they give. That constraint is what makes AI hard here — and it is the reason these products stuck.

  1. 01

    Human-in-the-loop by design, not as a disclaimer.

    Every generative workflow — recommendation letters, student profiling, session notes, parent communications — routes through review before anything is committed to a record. Counsellors keep professional judgement over every student-facing artefact. This is not a safety footnote; it is the specific thing institutional buyers screen for in procurement, and designing for it up front is why these features passed security review instead of stalling in it.

  2. 02

    Evaluation before rollout.

    Golden sets, model evaluation and quality thresholds a feature has to clear before it reaches users, plus guardrails and prompt-injection defence as standing requirements rather than incident response. A demo that impresses the leadership team and a feature that survives ten thousand real counsellor sessions are different products, and only one of them is measurable in advance.

  3. 03

    Outcome-gated roadmaps, not feature counts.

    I replaced feature-count roadmaps with an operating model where adoption metrics are instrumented first, experiments are structured, and rollout is gated on a named outcome moving. It is a slower-looking way to work that produces faster-compounding results — and it is the reason adoption numbers here are quotable at all.

  4. 04

    Compliance is a design input, not a legal review.

    The platform handles minor student records across 70+ jurisdictions — FERPA, COPPA, GDPR, DPDP — and admissions-adjacent features sit close to high-risk classification under the EU AI Act. I own that posture on the product side, which means these constraints shape the design at the whiteboard rather than arriving as a veto three weeks before launch.

  5. 05

    Unit economics from day one.

    Model routing and provider abstraction so the product is not hostage to one vendor's pricing, and inference cost treated as a product constraint with a margin attached. AI features that are beloved and unprofitable get switched off eventually; it is cheaper to design for the margin than to retrofit it.
